Build a prototype

Victor's prototypes, demos and tech showcases are made for one moment: a partner shows one to a customer who has never seen it. This page is the brief for the agent that builds it, and the review it must pass.

The brief for agents

Give the agent this brief with the task: paste it into the first message, or send it the address of the plain-text copy. It holds the rules on this page in short form and links to the details.

Who it's for

Build for the dumbest user possible: someone who knows nothing about the subject, has no interest in it and no time to work it out. That is the customer in the meeting, and sometimes the partner presenting too. At every step, without help, they can say what the prototype is doing, what it is worth and which key figure just moved.

A step that leaves one of these open needs a clearer label, a key figure or a sentence in the guide, or it goes. Keep the prototype lean, cut whatever the story doesn't need, and keep the case intact: the evidence behind every figure stays one click away. When the case needs more detail than a customer can take in, add a Customer view and a Full view, switched in the header.

Three things every prototype has

However lean a prototype gets, it keeps these three. They are on the keep list of every partner review.

Key figures

A customer should see at once what the case is worth. Show two to four key figures at the top right, chosen from these or defined for the case. The talk track explains each one in its How it works chapter, in a table row: the figure's name as on its tile, what it measures and its value.

From top to bottom

Victor's prototypes follow one layout, so a partner who has presented one finds their way around the next:

  1. The header: the name with its icon in a hexagon of its kind's colour, SIMULATION or LIVE in capitals, the How it works button and the theme toggle.
  2. One sentence under the header that says what the prototype does and whether its data is real.
  3. The key figures, top right.
  4. The work area, in the order of the story. Wherever it waits for a person, a bar right under what just happened says in words what to do next, such as “Your decision”, and holds the one primary action, which draws attention until it is pressed and stays in view however long the work area grows; a Next button alone is easily overlooked. The bar never covers what it asks about: at a decision, every option, doing nothing included, shows in full above it at 1280 × 720. A story whose steps show at once can play them by itself and stop where a person acts, as Presenting step by step sets out. Lilac marks what the AI generated, suggested or summarised, and nothing the agent prepares leaves the page until a person approves it. Records appear by name, never by the ids of the data, as Names, not record ids sets out, and short forms and names the room doesn't know get their words, as Short forms and unfamiliar names sets out. Where a live run with a model can start, “You are interacting with an AI system” stands in a lilac tag beside the control and stays while the run lasts, as Every live run says it is an AI sets out. Where the run starts by itself when the guide closes, that control says live in its name, such as “Run it live”, and stands after the steps, where nobody takes it for the way to start.
  5. The byline at the end of the page. A single-screen app that doesn't scroll carries it in the strip under the header instead.

Three steps, four minutes in all. Start on this screen.

The partner review

Before a prototype goes to a customer, Victor reviews it the way a partner of a consultancy would, and an agent runs the same review on its own work before calling it done. It takes the view of the dumbest user possible, in this order:

  1. Name the One Thing: one sentence on what the prototype shows the customer, with the key figure that proves it. If it takes two sentences, it is two prototypes, or the case isn't clear yet.
  2. Open it cold, in a new browser tab at 1280 × 720, with nobody explaining it. Note what the guide and the first screen make clear in the first ten seconds, and what they don't.
  3. Walk every step and say at each one what the prototype is doing, what it is worth and which key figure just moved. A step where one of the three is missing goes on the fix list, and so does any point where the page waits and you could miss what to press, and any word you would have to look up.
  4. Count the steps until a key figure first moves. Every step that doesn't move the story goes on the cut list.
  5. Check that the case survives the cuts: each figure comes from the prototype's own data, its evidence is one click away, and whatever is simulated or synthetic says so.
  6. Check the three things: the byline, the talk track that opens on start and explains every key figure, and the key figures at the top right.
  7. Look at the screens nobody demos: loading, an empty state, a failed live run, the other theme, 1920 × 1080 and the page opened from a file.
  8. Present it once along the guide's script, with a timer. The steps take the time the guide says they take.

The review ends with a verdict and three lists:

Partner review: <name>
Verdict: ready or not ready
The One Thing: <one sentence, with the key figure that proves it>
Keeps its promise: yes or no, with the evidence
Cut list: what goes, and why the case survives without it
Fix list: ranked; what, where and the direction of the fix
Keep list: the byline, the talk track, the key figures and whatever else must not be cut

There is no “almost ready”. A prototype with anything left on its fix list is not ready.

Before you share it

A prototype runs Vici's own checks over its pages. Clone the ViciDesign repository beside it and import them. Each returns the rules a page breaks, and an empty list when it keeps them.

// check-vici.mjs in the prototype's folder, with the ViciDesign clone beside it
import { readFileSync } from 'node:fs';
import { guideProblems } from '../ViciDesign/scripts/lib/guide.mjs';
import { bylineProblems } from '../ViciDesign/scripts/lib/byline.mjs';
import { writingProblems } from '../ViciDesign/scripts/lib/writing.mjs';
import { aiNoticeProblems } from '../ViciDesign/scripts/lib/ai.mjs';

const html = readFileSync('index.html', 'utf8');
const problems = [...guideProblems(html), ...bylineProblems(html), ...writingProblems(html), ...aiNoticeProblems(html)];
if (problems.length) { console.error(problems.join('\n')); process.exit(1); }
console.log('Vici checks passed');

The checks find broken rules. Only the partner review finds a prototype that keeps every rule and still doesn't make its case, so run it last.
