Guidelines

Data visualisation

Charts get their own colours. The categorical set changes lightness as well as hue, so series stay apart for readers with colour-vision deficiencies. The build measures this on every run. Marks use hexagons where other systems use dots, and part-to-whole data becomes a honeycomb waffle instead of a pie.

Palettes

  • Categorical (--vc-data-1…6): merlot, teal, apricot, fjord, lilac, honey, always in this order. --vc-data-7 is grey for “other”, --vc-data-8 a darker grey for baselines and previous periods. In light mode the marks are deep jewel tones that reach 3:1 against white. In dark mode they turn to pastels.
  • Pastel fills (--vc-data-pastel-1…8): the same families as soft fills, for areas behind a strong line, highlighted bands and backgrounds of annotations.
  • Sequential (--vc-seq-1…7): one merlot ramp from blush to the base colour, for heat maps and choropleths.
  • Diverging (--vc-div-1…7): coral below the midpoint, fjord above, linen in the middle. Blue against red-orange stays distinguishable for the common forms of colour blindness.

Examples

All five charts are plain SVG generated by the build, with colours from CSS variables. Switch the theme and they follow. The same functions produce the charts in the dashboard starter.

Anatomy

  • Title says what the chart shows, subtitle the unit and period. Put both in the <figcaption> of a .vc-chart.
  • Gridlines use --vc-chart-grid, horizontal only, and sit behind the data. Axis labels use --vc-chart-axis at 11 px with tabular figures.
  • Size: draw a chart at the size it is shown and give the SVG that width and height, so 11 px stays 11 px. Wrap charts with text in .vc-chart__plot: on phones they scroll sideways instead of shrinking their labels. Charts without text, like the waffle and the hexbin map, can simply fill the frame.
  • Markers are flat-top hexagons with a 1.5 px ring in the surface colour, so overlapping points stay separate. Enlarge the most recent point.
  • Legends (.vc-legend) use hexagon swatches in the series order. Label lines directly at their ends whenever there is room.
  • Source goes underneath in caption size: .vc-chart__source.

Rules

Do

Use at most six categorical colours. Group the rest into “other” in --vc-data-7, or split the chart into small multiples.

Don't

Don't use pies or donuts. Angles are hard to compare and circles don't belong in Vici. Use a sorted bar chart, or a hexagon waffle for a single part-to-whole.

Do

Start bar charts at zero, sort categories by value unless they have a natural order, and keep units in the subtitle instead of on every tick.

Don't

Don't use the status colours (sage, coral, honey) as series colours. A coral line will be read as “bad”. Use them only when the data really is good or bad.

  • Show good and bad trends with colour and direction: a sage tag with an upward arrow, a coral tag with a downward arrow (see Stat card).
  • Highlight one series by keeping it in its colour and turning the others to --vc-data-7.
  • Offer the numbers as a table, too. A chart is a summary, not an accessible replacement for the data.
