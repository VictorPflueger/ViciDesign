Byline and profile links

Every prototype, demo and website made with Vici, Victor's personal site included, says who built it in the same way: a small byline with his portrait in a hexagon, a link to his LinkedIn profile and a link to his website. This page covers the byline, profile links in footers and contact sections, other brands' logos, the link rules and the portrait.

Profiles: LinkedIn, then GitHub Other brands' logos stay unchanged

Every surface

Every prototype, demo and website made with Vici ends with the byline, Victor's personal site and these docs included. Its look may change between versions. What it does stays the same:

  • it says “Built by” (“Erstellt von” on a German surface) and names Victor Pflüger;
  • it links to his LinkedIn profile and to his website, both with rel="author";
  • the website link, which shows only a globe, has the accessible name “Victor Pflüger's website” and the tooltip “Website”, and is at least 24 × 24 px.

The addresses live in one file, src/brand/profile.json. The build writes them into these docs, the starters and the Streamlit helper, so when the website moves to its own domain, one line changes. A surface that can't hold links, such as a Power BI report, has a footer that reads “Built by Victor Pflüger” followed by the website address.

npm run check fails when a docs page, a starter or the Streamlit helper has no byline, or when either link is missing. A prototype checks its own page with bylineProblems(html) from scripts/lib/byline.mjs.

Byline

The byline goes at the end of a page or app: in the footer, under a dashboard, at the bottom of a prototype's sidebar. It reads “Built by”, then the portrait, the name and LinkedIn's logo as one link to LinkedIn, then a globe that links to the website. The first link gives one generous target and one clear name for screen readers, “Victor Pflüger on LinkedIn”. The globe's name is “Victor Pflüger's website”, and its tooltip reads “Website”.

LinkedIn's logo is an image in black. In the dark theme, and in dark sections such as a brand footer, .vc-platform-mark shows it in white, which is LinkedIn's own white version.

With several profiles

When the byline links to more than one profile, the name stops being a link and each profile gets its own, and the website keeps its link too: as words where there's room, or as logos where there isn't. Word links need nothing else. Logo links need an aria-label that names the person and the platform, and they keep a target of at least 24 px around the logo.

On the personal site

Victor\'s personal site carries the byline too, at the end of every page like any other surface; there the globe links to the site\'s own home page. Its footer also lists the profiles, and its contact section turns them into buttons. Those links, unlike the byline\'s, use rel="me" (see the link rules). A platform's logo goes on secondary or ghost buttons, where .vc-platform-mark picks the right one of black and white. A primary button would need the opposite colour, so keep primary buttons to words.

Other brands' logos

A link to a profile may show the platform's logo, but the logo belongs to the platform, and its owner decides how it looks. Vici therefore uses the owners' files exactly as they publish them. The hexagon rule and the palette apply to Vici's own shapes only.

Do
  • Use the platform's own file, unchanged.
  • Keep it black on light surfaces and white on dark ones. The .vc-platform-mark class does this for inline SVG and for image files.
  • Show LinkedIn's at 21 px or more and GitHub's at 18 px, with space around it.
  • Use it only to link to your own profile on that platform.
Don't
  • Redraw a platform's logo, or use a Vici icon in its place.
  • Colour it merlot, pastel or with a gradient.
  • Put it inside a hexagon, circle or square of your own.
  • Build it into a lockup with your name or logo.

Link rules

  • Name the platform. The link text or its aria-label says whose profile it is and where: “Victor Pflüger on LinkedIn”. A logo alone isn't a name.
  • One link per destination. If the name and the logo go to the same profile, they're one link. Don't place two links to the same place next to each other.
  • Keep the same order everywhere. LinkedIn, GitHub, the personal site, then email or anything else.
  • Mark the relationship. On your personal site, links to your own profiles get rel="me": it says that both pages belong to the same person, which is how profile verification works on Mastodon and in the IndieWeb. Anywhere else, such as a prototype, a dashboard or these docs, the page isn't you but your work, so a footer link gets rel="author". The byline keeps rel="author" everywhere, the personal site included.
  • Don't link to the project's own repository. A prototype, dashboard or site built with Vici doesn't link to its own GitHub repository, unless that is specifically required, for example because people are meant to install or contribute. Link to the GitHub profile instead. Links to other projects, such as the prototypes listed on a personal site, are fine.
  • Stay in the same tab unless leaving would lose work, as in a prototype with unsaved input. Then add target="_blank", noopener in the link's rel, the external icon on word links, and “(opens in a new tab)” in the link's accessible name.
  • Use the canonical addresses, https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorpflueger and https://github.com/VictorPflueger, without tracking parameters.
  • The website address comes from src/brand/profile.json. For the time being it is . Link to exactly this address, and change it only in that file when the site moves to its own domain.

On a personal site, also list the profiles in the page's structured data, so search engines connect them to the name:

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Person",
  "name": "Victor Pflüger",
  "url": "",
  "sameAs": ["https://www.linkedin.com/in/victorpflueger", "https://github.com/VictorPflueger"]
}
</script>

Portrait

  • assets/brand/portrait.jpg is the original photo, 640 × 640 px, for large avatars such as a page hero. assets/brand/portrait-96.jpg is a small copy for bylines and small avatars, made by scripts/brand/portrait.py.
  • To replace it, use another square photo of 640 px or more, so it stays sharp in a hero, and run the script again. The avatar is a flat-top hexagon: it trims a sliver at the top and bottom and cuts the left and right corners, so frame head and shoulders centrally.
  • The alt text is empty (alt="") when the name is right next to the portrait, as in the byline. On its own, describe it: “Portrait of Victor Pflüger”.
  • Without the photo, use the initials in an avatar, or a monogram tile from the logo page.
  • The portrait belongs to Victor Pflüger's personal brand and isn't covered by the MIT licence of the code.
