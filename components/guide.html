How it works guide

Every prototype, demo, challenge and showcase opens with a short guide, its standard talk track: what it does, how it works and how to present it. Someone who only gets the link still understands it.

How the prototype works

Shows the 128 invoices the agent couldn't match

Atlas is a sample prototype. Every morning an agent matches supplier invoices to their orders and leaves the rest for a person, who works through them on this page.

Left for a person128

Four steps, five minutes in all. Start on the exception list.

Title

The title says what kind of thing the guide explains. The words are fixed, so every guide is recognisable at a glance.

Guide titles
KindEnglishGerman
PrototypeHow the prototype worksSo funktioniert der Prototyp
DemoHow the demo worksSo funktioniert die Demo
ChallengeHow the challenge worksSo funktioniert die Challenge
ShowcaseHow the showcase worksSo funktioniert der Showcase

The header button that reopens the guide reads “How it works” (“So funktioniert's” in German), with the info icon and visible text. Pages that are none of these four kinds, such as the personal site, these docs and the landing starter, have no guide.

Chapters

A guide has three to eight chapters, and four to six read best. The first two and the last are fixed; the chapters between them are yours. Each chapter has a short label in the rail, the list of chapters beside the content.

Chapter order
PositionChapter idRail labelAnswers
FirstwhatWhat it doesWhat it is, who it is for and which question it answers
SecondhowHow it worksInputs, logic and outputs, and the key figures: a table with one row per figure, what it measures and its value. See Key figures
BetweenYour ownUp to five wordsOptional: what is simulated and what is real, results, how to read the page, limits. “How” can run over several chapters.
LastpresentHow to presentThe presenter's path, described under The presenting chapter

Copy rules

  • The heading is one claim in sentence case, at most 60 characters, and specific to the product: a number or a noun from the prototype. No questions and no colon kickers.
  • The lead is one or two sentences, at most 40 words.
  • The body is one figure, table or short list of at most five items.
  • Numbers come from the prototype's own data. An app that computes its figures fills them in when it receives vc:guidechange.
  • No eyebrow and no “Chapter 2 of 5” label: the rail and the footer already say where you are.
  • Plain words in British English or German, following the Writing rules.

Every visible word is in the markup, so a German prototype writes its own. These attributes do the work:

Guide attributes
AttributeOnDoes
data-vc-guideDialogMakes the dialog a guide
data-vc-guide-autoDialogOpens it by itself; see Opening
data-vc-guide-countDialogThe footer count, with {n} and {total}; “{n} of {total}” by default
data-vc-guide-railDialogThe accessible name of the rail, the list of chapters; “Chapters” by default
data-vc-chapterSectionThe chapter id: what, how, present or your own
data-vc-navSectionThe chapter's label in the rail
data-vc-guide-back, data-vc-guide-nextFooter buttonsMove to the previous or next chapter
data-vc-guide-doneNext buttonIts label on the last chapter, where it closes the guide; “Start” by default
data-vc-guide-openAny buttonOpens the guide with that id
data-vc-guide-chapterThe same buttonOpens it at a chapter, by id or number

From a script, Vici.guide.open('guide', 'present') opens a guide at a chapter and Vici.guide.show('guide', 2) moves an open guide to another one. The dialog fires vc:guidechange with the chapter's id and index, and vc:guideclose with its returnValue.

In German

A German prototype writes every word in German, the built-in ones included: the count, the rail's name and the buttons. lang="de" on the dialog tells screen readers to switch language.

Behaviour

  • The guide is a modal dialog: Escape and the close button close it, focus stays inside, and closing returns focus to whatever opened it.
  • When it opens, focus goes to Next. On every change of chapter, focus moves to the new chapter's heading, so a screen reader reads each chapter once. There is no live region.
  • From 900 px the rail lists the chapters with numbers in hexagons. Below that it shrinks to a strip of numbers, and at 600 px and below the guide fills the screen.
  • The rail's items are buttons, and the current one has aria-current="step".
  • The footer shows the count in tabular figures, Back, which is disabled on the first chapter, and Next. On the last chapter Next closes the guide.
  • Chapters change without animation, and the hexagon markers keep their outlines in Windows contrast themes.

Opening

  • With data-vc-guide-auto, the guide opens by itself the first time a prototype loads in a browser tab, and not again when that tab reloads. If the browser blocks storage, it opens on every load.
  • ?guide=0 in the address keeps it closed, for a presenter who has already explained the prototype.
  • ?guide=present opens it at the presenting chapter. Any chapter id or number works the same way.
  • The header button reopens it at the first chapter.

The presenting chapter

The last chapter is a script for whoever shows the prototype, Victor or anyone he sends the link to.

  • It holds three to seven steps in an ol.vc-guide__path.
  • Each step names the control or view to show, in bold, then one sentence to say, in quotation marks, then its time in .vc-guide__time, such as “1 min”.
  • When the sentence holds markup, such as figures a script fills in, it goes in one span.vc-guide__say, so the path keeps it whole.
  • The lead gives the total time, and the steps add up to it.
  • If the prototype has a demo script, this chapter condenses it.
  • The last button closes the guide, so the page behind it should already show the first step. Its label comes from data-vc-guide-done.

Key figures

A prototype shows two to four key figures at the top of the page, usually top right, so a customer sees at once what the case delivers: money gained, lost or saved, lead time, time from detection to decision, session cost (measured in a live run, estimated in a simulated one), people affected, performance against a benchmark or a score. Each is a vc-stat tile that carries its name in data-vc-kpi. Build a prototype has an example of each.

  • The How it works chapter explains every key figure in a table, one row each: the figure's name as on its tile, what it measures and its value. Where the numbers come from and what is simulated go in a chapter of their own, such as “What is real”.
  • The presenting path points at the figures when they move, so the talk track says what each one shows.
  • A figure that changes during a demo starts from a value that makes sense before anything happens, such as “0 of 12”, rather than an empty tile.

Check

npm run check reads the guides of the app, dashboard and blank starters and fails when one breaks these rules: the title, the order and number of chapters, one heading, lead and body in each, the length limits, a path of three to seven timed steps with a sentence to say, a header button with visible text, and a How it works chapter with a row for every key figure marked data-vc-kpi, saying what it measures and its value. npm run check:render opens each starter in a browser and tests the behaviour and opening rules above.

A prototype checks its own guide the same way. guideProblems(html) from scripts/lib/guide.mjs returns the rules a page breaks, none when it follows them, and node scripts/check-guide-render.mjs http://localhost:8000/ runs the browser test against a prototype served over http.
